A conditional reduction of the L3 double-log Navier–Stokes blow-up rate

Paper: Robinson, P. — A reduction of the L³ double-logarithmic blow-up rate for the Navier–Stokes equations to a quantitative resonance-cancellation estimate Format: 10 pp, LaTeX, single author, "Independent" Dated: 10 May 2026 Reviewer: team-willie

One-line summary

Paul's paper announces a conditional reduction of a named open problem in 3D Navier–Stokes regularity theory — the "L3 double-log" conjecture in the lineage of Tao 2019 — to a single quantitative bilinear estimate (Hypothesis G). He does not prove the open problem; he proves "if G holds, then the double-log bound follows", and isolates G as the new open problem.

What this is not: a proof of the Clay Millennium Problem. Not even a proof of the double-log bound. It's a structural reduction.

What this is: the kind of paper that, if the L1→L12 chain checks out under refereeing, would be a legitimate advance in the Tao 2019 programme — publishable in ARMA, Annals of PDE, or similar. Above the "amateur Navier–Stokes" line by a wide margin; below the "proof of Clay" line by an equally wide margin.

§1 The lineage — why this matters

To anchor what's happening:

  1. Clay Millennium Problem (2000, $1M). Does a smooth solution to the 3D incompressible Navier–Stokes equations stay smooth forever, or can it develop a singularity in finite time?
  2. Escauriaza–Seregin–Šverák (2003). If a solution blows up at time T*, its L3 norm must diverge. So L3 is the "critical" detector for blow-up.
  3. Tao (2019, arXiv:1908.04958). Gave a quantitative version — if blow-up occurs, the L3 norm must grow at least like a triple-iterated logarithm:
    A ≥ c · ( log log log ( 1 / (T* − t) ) )c
  4. The natural next conjecture — "double log". Improve "triple log" to "double log":
    A ≥ c · ( log log ( 1 / (T* − t) ) )c ⇔ A ≥ exp( exp( c · A−c ) )
    Widely conjectured. Unproved. This is the milestone Paul targets — well short of the full Clay problem, but the natural next step after Tao 2019.
Where Paul sits: Tao's triple-log proof has three nested pigeonhole layers — frequency, time-slab, and Carleman back-propagation. To reach double-log you must collapse one of these without forfeiting the other two. The frequency layer is structurally rigid in any L3-critical bookkeeping. So one of the other two must be saved. Paul's route saves the time-slab layer by exploiting a kinematic cancellation on the resonant remainder of Bony's paraproduct decomposition.

§2 What he claims — Theorem 1, the "reduction"

The convective term u·∇u in Navier–Stokes is split using Bony's paraproduct decomposition (a standard 1981 harmonic-analysis technique):

u · ∇u = Tu(∇u) + T∇u(u) + R(u, ∇u)

The first two pieces ("paraproducts") are estimated cleanly — no logarithmic loss. The only source of a stray log in Tao's proof is the third piece, the diagonal resonant remainder R(u, ∇u). If you can save that one log, triple-log → double-log.

Theorem 1 (Reduction).
Assume Hypothesis G (the resonance-cancellation gain). Then any smooth solution of the 3D Navier–Stokes equations satisfying ‖u‖L∞tL3x ≤ A and blowing up at T* satisfies the double-logarithmic bound A ≥ exp(exp(c · A−c)) for an absolute c > 0.

The proof is a 12-step chain L1 → L12, summarised in §3 of the paper. Eleven of the twelve steps use only standard, properly cited tools. The single non-standard input is G.

L1
Setup
Koch–Tataru well-posedness fixes the back-propagation interval [T0, T*) with δA = c·A−4.
L2
Bony decomposition
Split u·∇u into two paraproducts + one resonant remainder.
L3
High–low paraproduct
Lemma 2: no log loss on the two paraproduct pieces. Standard Bernstein + Bahouri–Chemin–Danchin §2.97.
L4
Pressure recovery
Lemma 3: p = RiRj(uiuj) decomposed paraproduct-style; resonant block feeds L6.
L5
Standard resonant estimate
Lemma 4: the one-log loss in Tao's architecture lives here.
L6 ★
Resonance-cancellation gain
The only step that invokes G. Buys back a half-derivative with residual loss ε(A) ≤ A−η.
L7
Closure inversion
Lemma 5: algebraic inequality 2C·√cKT·A−1−η·log A ≤ c* — verified numerically for A ≥ 100.
L8
Frequency-localised bootstrap
Duhamel mild solution on a single dyadic shell N*; the unique pigeonhole log A factor in the whole proof.
L9
Backward propagation
Single application of Tao 2019 Lemma 3.1 — converts smallness at T* to smallness at T0 at cost eCAc.
L10
Log-count accounting
L8 contributes 1 log + L9 contributes 1 application + L6 saves 1 log = 2 iterated exponentials. The whole game.
L11

Tao 2019 §5: local quantitative bound patches to global lim-sup without further log loss.
L12
Conclude
Combine with ESS 2003 + subcritical reduction → A ≥ exp(exp(c·A−c)). □

Read this carefully: the load-bearing step is L10 (Log-count accounting). The whole reduction stands or falls on whether the bookkeeping in that step actually closes — i.e., whether the saving from L6 truly compensates the cost from L8+L9. A referee's first hour is spent on L10.

§3 Hypothesis G — qualitative known, quantitative open

Hypothesis G (the resonance-cancellation gain).
There exists an absolute η > 0 such that, for every divergence-free Schwartz field u on ℝ³ and every dyadic frequency N,
‖PN ℙ R(u, ∇u)‖L² ≤ C · N−1/2+ε(A) · Σj ≥ N/8 N3/2 ‖Pju‖²L², ε(A) ≤ C′·A−η
where ℙ is the Leray projector onto divergence-free fields and A = ‖u‖L∞tL3.

The mechanism — kinematic cancellation

On the resonant pairs (frequencies j ≈ k), the symbol of the symmetrised pair Pju · ∇Pku + Pku · ∇Pju contains the factor (η + (ξ−η))·û(η) û(ξ−η). The divergence-free constraint ξ·û(ξ) = 0 forces this to vanish to first order in the sum-frequency direction at the diagonal pair (j=j). After Leray projection, this becomes a free half-derivative on R(u, ∇u).

The qualitative form of this gain is textbook (Bahouri–Chemin–Danchin §3.2). What is open is the precise A-dependence of the residual at the off-diagonal pairs (j, j±1) and (j, j±2), where the symmetrisation is incomplete. That's what G isolates.

Why "absolute η" is the right target

Paul splits ℙR(u, ∇u) = ℙRdiag + ℙRoff. The half-derivative gain is sharp on the diagonal block; the loss ε(A) in G comes entirely from Roff. A-dependence enters via Bernstein constants in the mixed-Lebesgue partition at L³ criticality. The residual off-diagonal question becomes a single Calderón–Zygmund / paraproduct-calculus question — well-posed and structurally familiar to harmonic-analysis experts.

§4 Numerical evidence — clever and load-bearing

Paul models the compounded log-cost over K = ⌊log A⌋ bootstrap iterations as:

C(A) = (1 − ε(A))K · (log A)²

Target for double-log: C(A) ~ log A · log log A. Standard Tao 2019 cost (no saving): C(A) ~ (log A)². He tabulates three regimes for ε(A):

Reading the table: only the constant-η regime gives a bounded ratio against the target — i.e., the saving log survives the bootstrap. Both decaying regimes (η ~ 1/log A or 1/(log A)²) collapse to triple-log behaviour. The ratio C(A)/(log A · log log A) reaches 9.48 at A = 10¹⁵ in the (log A)² regime — Paul calls this the script's "failure threshold."

The methodological point: this rules out a soft, "qualitative" version of G — any ε(A) that decays with A is insufficient. The correct quantitative target is absolute η > 0. That's not a convenience choice; the table forces it.

He also does an algebraic cross-check on the L7 closure inequality (margin ≈ 0.1 for all A ≥ 100, well above Koch–Tataru smallness). The duration factor √δA = A−2 dominates the A1−η bilinear amplification by A1+η/log A.

§5 Three competing approaches, one barrier — the most interesting meta-claim

This is the part I'd flag as the most novel meta-observation in the paper. Paul sketches three independent technique families for the same double-log target, and shows each reduces to the same absolute-rate-class barrier ε(A) ≤ A−η:

Paul is explicit that he is not claiming constant-level equivalence — the four hypotheses may have different content. He claims only that the same shape of barrier (ε(A) ≤ A−η, η > 0 absolute) appears across four independent approaches.

If correct, this is the deeper structural observation in the paper. It suggests the absolute-η barrier is a real, method-independent obstruction to the double-log conjecture — not an artifact of Paul's specific Bony-paraproduct choice. Experts (Tao, Šverák, Vasseur) would scrutinise this claim carefully; it could be right, or it could be that the four reductions look superficially similar but in fact have distinct content. Either resolution is valuable.

§6 NS-fingerprint discharge — competent self-policing

Tao's 2016 result on the averaged 3D Navier–Stokes equation gave finite-time blow-up for a modified system. This is the classical graveyard for false Navier–Stokes "proofs" — any conditional reduction of the regularity problem must not also accidentally apply to the averaged model, or else it would contradict Tao 2016.

Paul addresses this explicitly. The Bony decomposition fails to commute with the rotation projections of Tao's averaging operator B̃: writing Πα for the αth rotation projection,

Πα R(Pju, Pku) ≠ R(ΠαPju, ΠαPku)

because rotation does not preserve the dyadic shell selection |j − k| ≤ 2. The non-commutator sum destroys the half-derivative cancellation underlying L6. So Paul's reduction is genuinely NS-specific in the sense of Tao 2016.

Why this matters for credibility: most amateur Navier–Stokes attempts skip this check, and most of them die on Tao 2016. Paul shows he knows the graveyard exists and walks around it.

What's right

  • Every borrowed tool is correctly cited: Bahouri–Chemin–Danchin, Bony, CKN, ESS, Koch–Tataru, Tao 2019, Vasseur, Jia–Šverák, Kenig–Koch, NRS.
  • The framing is honest: "conditional reduction", "the single non-standard ingredient is G", "we do not claim mutual reducibility at the constant level". No overclaiming.
  • The numerical setup correctly identifies that absolute η > 0 is required — not just qualitatively ε(A) → 0 but a sharp rate.
  • The NS-fingerprint discharge is the right sanity check and is not skipped.

What needs careful refereeing

  • L10 — the log-count accounting. The whole reduction stands or falls on whether the bookkeeping in this step actually closes. A careful referee will rebuild the constants by hand and check that the saving from L6 truly compensates the cost from L8+L9.
  • The "four reductions, one barrier" claim (Appendix A). Experts will want to see whether the four hypotheses are structurally the same shape or genuinely equivalent at the constant level. The latter is a much stronger and more interesting claim.
  • "Independent" author, no arXiv post. Not disqualifying — Yitang Zhang was at a hotel chain when he proved bounded gaps — but the paper hasn't gone through normal preprint norms yet.
  • Hypothesis G itself is hard. Calderón–Zygmund sharpness questions at L³ criticality are unsolved for good reasons. Even if Theorem 1 is correct, G might never get proved.

§8 Workflow signal — read the Acknowledgements

One line in the paper jumps out beyond the math:

"The numerical scripts and claim audit underlying §5 were produced under an automated multi-cycle workflow with Lean elaboration as an external verifier; the workflow is described separately. The author thanks anonymous referees of the project's documentation for sharpening the formulation of G."

Three flags I'd surface:

  1. "Automated multi-cycle workflow" — Paul has built (or is using) an agent loop that produces math research output.
  2. "Lean elaboration as an external verifier" — Lean is the formal-proof-assistant gold standard. If parts of L1→L12 have actually been elaborated in Lean, that's a significant credibility multiplier: the human-checker work shrinks to "does the Lean spec match the informal theorem statement".
  3. "Anonymous referees of the project's documentation" — phrased oddly. Almost certainly means internal automated reviewers (other agents) reviewed the formulation of G, not external human referees. Consistent with an agent-pipeline output.
What this means: this paper appears to be the output of Paul's research-agent system applied to a named open problem in mathematics, with Lean used as the verifier. If so, the system is at least as much the story as the paper. A workflow that produces credible conditional reductions of Clay-adjacent problems is a serious capability.

§10 Draft reply to Paul

Read it. Clear what it does and what it doesn't — a conditional reduction of Tao 2019's triple-log → double-log to a sharp L³-critical resonance-cancellation estimate (your Hypothesis G), with a clean log-count argument and a nice three-class structural observation that the absolute-η barrier seems to be the load-bearing slot across Carleman / concentration-compactness / De Giorgi as well as your Bony route. The NS-fingerprint discharge against Tao's averaged model is the right self-check; the η-regime table is the right way to show absolute-η is the correct quantitative target.

Three things I'd want to understand more before forming a view:

  1. Is Hypothesis G (or any meaningful subset of L1→L12) actually elaborated in Lean, or is "Lean elaboration as external verifier" load-bearing on the workflow rather than this paper specifically?
  2. The "four reductions, one barrier" claim — is that structural analogy, or did you (or your pipeline) attempt explicit constant-level equivalence between, say, the Bony η and the Carleman η?
  3. Plan for the off-diagonal residual subproblem (eq. 23) — is that a Calderón–Zygmund T(1) question, or something more bespoke at L³ criticality?

Workflow-level: are you posting to arXiv, or circulating privately first? If this is a typical output of your multi-cycle system applied to a named open problem, the system itself is the story.
